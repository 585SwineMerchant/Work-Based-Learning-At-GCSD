Work-Based Learning

Authentic learning happens in more than one place.

Work-based learning is the full continuum of experiences that connect students to real careers, workplace expectations, authentic audiences, and increasingly independent responsibility. An internship-style placement is one important form of WBL—but it is not the only one.

One continuum · multiple forms

The proposal expands WBL; it does not introduce it.

The Culinary Pathway already brings careers and workplace expectations into school and sends students into the community for career-connected experiences. The proposed senior course adds a sustained, internship-style experience at an approved off-site workplace.

01 · Current WBL

Explore careers and workplaces

Guest professionals, demonstrations, restaurant and workplace exposure, career conversations, and certification preparation connect instruction to the industry.

02 · Current authentic work

Serve real audiences at school

Catering, pop-ups, client communication, menu planning, costing, production, service, feedback, and school-based enterprise create genuine workplace expectations and documented WBL hours.

03 · Proposed WBL expansion

Learn at an approved worksite

A senior cooperative course adds sustained off-site placement, individual training goals, verified hours, coordinator contact, employer supervision and evaluation, reflection, and a capstone portfolio.

What makes every form WBL

The setting changes. The learning design remains.

ConnectedEach experience links curriculum to careers, industry practice, or authentic workplace expectations.
IntentionalStudents know what they are learning, practicing, and expected to demonstrate.
DocumentedParticipation, hours, work products, feedback, and reflection make growth visible.
ProgressiveCareer awareness grows into preparation, authentic responsibility, and—when approved—supervised placement.
